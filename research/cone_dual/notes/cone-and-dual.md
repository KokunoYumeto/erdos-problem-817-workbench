## 1. Results and the precise remaining quantifier

Write S(A)=sum(A). A canonical pair has integer b>S(A); its actual fifth-arity digit set D=H_5(A) is contained in [0,4(b-1)]. Let M(A,b) denote the predecessor's seven-coordinate numerical-image matrix. Its coordinates, in their original order, are

\[
\mathcal R=(\{0\},\{0,1\},\{0,2\},\{0,1,2\},
             \{0,3\},\{0,1,3\},\{0,1,2,3\}),
\quad F_R(Y)=|Y+R|.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The present contribution proves the following.

**Theorem C1 (exact feasible cone and integer monoid).** The closure of the cone generated by F(Y), for finite centrally symmetric numerical sets Y, has exactly nine facets and ten extremal rays, all displayed below. Its integral points form the monoid generated by those ten primitive rays. An explicit nonnegative integral section is obtained by routing a two-node flow. Its original ray presentation has kernel of rank three.

**Theorem C2 (positive lifting with the composition defect retained).** Every actual seven-coordinate digit matrix has a nonnegative integral 10 by 10 lift. The lift intertwines the original seven-coordinate action. Its source-kernel action and the defect of the chosen lifts under composition have explicit unique integral primitives. The defect obeys a stated cocycle identity. The observable norm of every lifted word product is exactly its numerical image count; no additional leading spectral growth is introduced by the lift.

**Theorem C3 (finite rational certificates below the global rate).** Let

\[
 \lambda_k=\lim_{n\to\infty}g_k(n)^{1/n}.
\]

For every u<lambda_k with u>0 there is a strictly positive, rational, piecewise-linear, concave homogeneous function g on the actual feasible cone such that

\[
 g(M(A,b)x)\ge u^{|A|}g(x)
\tag{1.2}
\]

for every canonical pair whose actual binary image is modular-k-admissible. The number of pieces and the coefficient denominators have an explicit bound depending only on the strict gap, not on |A|, b, or k. Conversely, any such universally verified certificate proves u<=lambda_k. Checking one given arithmetic pair reduces to finitely many rational cone inequalities. The universal quantifier over the arithmetic pairs is still present.

**Theorem C4 (an exact switching application).** For the actual pairs

\[
 X:\ (A,b)=(\{3,13\},23),\qquad

\tag{1.3}
\]

all binary concatenations are five-admissible. The least possible lower limiting fifth-image growth per level over every infinite switching sequence is sqrt(285), attained by alternating X,Y. Both individual homogeneous image growth rates exceed sqrt(285). A two-piece function proves the uniform lower bound. These are different observations with the exact image-to-arithmetic conversion recalled in Section 9. This example is not a new global upper record.

**Theorem C5 (the observation does not decide original admissibility).** The two rank-four pairs (31,{1,3,4,7}) and (31,{1,2,4,8}) have the same complete seven-coordinate matrix. ## 2. Original binary windows and the supported zero loop

For a finite numerical set Y, consider the literal length-four window at t:

\[
 w_t=(1_Y(t),1_Y(t-1),1_Y(t-2),1_Y(t-3)).
\]

For nonempty Y retain all windows with min(Y)<=t<=max(Y)+3. Additional zero padding is an optional recorded parameter. In this finite interval every word in {0,1}^4 is an admitted label, including 0000. Its count is finite. Extending both padding ends by p adds exactly 2p to that word's coefficient and changes no other word count. An active empty Y may be represented on the fixed interval [0,3], giving four copies of 0000. Global absence is a different support label.

Index a word by e=sum_(i=0)^3 w_i2^i. It is an edge from e>>1 to e mod 8 in the binary de Bruijn graph on the eight three-bit vertices. The original word path begins and ends at vertex 000, so its edge-count vector z satisfies

\[
 \partial z=0,
 \qquad \partial e_w=e_{w\bmod8}-e_{w>>1}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

If Y is centrally symmetric, reflection t->min(Y)+max(Y)+3-t reverses the four-bit word. Thus z_w=z_(rev w).

Let Z_1^+ be this reflection-fixed integral cycle lattice. The superscript denotes the fixed sublattice of the reflection involution, not a claim that all its coefficients are nonnegative. Its dimension is eight. The numerical observation is the integral map

\[
 J:Z_1^+\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^7,
 \qquad (Jz)_R=\sum_w [\exists i\in R:w_i=1]\,z_w.
\tag{2.2}
\]

For the literal source, Jz=F(Y). Word 0000 maps to zero under J but is a nonzero cycle in Z_1^+. Its coefficient remains the original padding observation.

### 3.1 Explicit nonnegative decomposition

Equations (2.4) are a two-node flow: u,g enter the first node, v leaves it, w goes to the second node, h enters the second, and e,f leave it. The coordinates d,i are independent loops.

Retain the following coefficients, without rescaling x:

\[
 a=\min(u,v),\quad b=v-a,\quad u'=u-a,\quad g'=g-b,
\]
\[
 c=\min(h,e),\quad d'=h-c,\quad e'=e-c,\quad f'=f-d',
\]
\[
 p=\min(u',e'),\quad q=u'-p,\quad r=e'-p,\quad s=g'-r.
\]

The balance equations prove nonnegativity of every number and the remaining identity s=f'-q. The exact decomposition is

\[
\boxed{x=a r_1+p r_2+q r_3+b r_4+r r_5+s r_6+d r_7+c r_8+d' r_9+i r_{10}.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus K is generated by the ten displayed rays. Integral x has integral pattern coordinates and hence integral coefficients in (3.2). Consequently

\[
\boxed{\mathscr K\cap\mathbb Z^7=\sum_{j=1}^{10}\mathbb N r_j.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This is an exact Hilbert-basis statement for the local circulation monoid. It does not assert that every integer point is the observation of one finite numerical set.

Each ray is extreme: the table rows vanishing on it have rank six, and their sum is zero on that ray and strictly positive on all other displayed rays. ## 4. Exact positive integer lifts and their original relations

For a symmetric actual digit set D and base b with 0<=D<=4(b-1), the predecessor's literal residue decomposition gives

\[
 F(D+bY)=M(D,b)F(Y).
\tag{4.1}
\]

For completeness, in a row R let E_R=D+R. For each present residue z retain

\[
 Q_z=\{(e-z)/b:e\in E_R,\ e\equiv z\pmod b\},\quad h_z=\min Q_z.
\]

The numerical union is the disjoint union of z+bh_z+b(Y+Q_z-h_z). Its inverse on that residue fibre is the displayed integer quotient. Reflection pairs the two actual three-point shapes that share one count in (1.1). The nonnegative integer row records the number of residues giving each original representative shape.

Since D+bY remains centrally symmetric, (4.1) and the explicit density (3.4) prove

\[
 M\mathscr K\subseteq\mathscr K.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Let Gamma be the seven-by-ten integer matrix whose columns are r_j. Let s_+ be the specified positive section (3.2). Define

\[
 \widehat M e_j=s_+(Mr_j).
\]

Then \widehat M is nonnegative integral and

\[
\boxed{\Gamma\widehat M=M\Gamma.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

The section is a set map, not a claimed additive inverse to Gamma.

### 4.1 The kernel is retained over the integers

The full integral sequence is

\[
\boxed{0\to\mathbb Z^3\xrightarrow B\mathbb Z^{10}
       \xrightarrow\Gamma\mathbb Z^7\to0,}
\tag{4.4}
\]

where B has columns

\[
 b_1=(-1,1,0,1,-1,0,0,0,0,0),
\]
\[

\]
\[
 b_3=(0,0,0,0,-1,1,0,1,-1,0).
\]

Indeed a kernel vector is uniquely determined by its coordinates 2,3,8 (one-based); inserting them as the coefficients of these columns reconstructs all other coordinates from the two-node source flow. Surjectivity of Gamma follows integrally as well: for any integer x, adding a sufficiently large multiple of the interior point (8,12,12,14,12,14,15) puts x in K. Apply (3.3) to that point and subtract its original lift. This proves every assertion in (4.4), not just a rational rank computation.

Because of (4.3), there is a unique integer A_M with

\[
 \widehat M B=BA_M.
\]

The selected lifts need not compose exactly. Define the unique integer primitive D(M,N) by

\[
\boxed{\widehat M\widehat N-\widehat{MN}=B D(M,N).}
\tag{4.5}
\]

It is computed columnwise by the same original coordinate extraction. Associativity, followed by injectivity of B, proves the full cocycle identity

\[
\boxed{D(M,N)\widehat P+D(MN,P)
 =A_M D(N,P)+D(M,NP).}
\tag{4.6}
\]

The difference is genuinely nonzero. For M belonging to (A,b)=({3,10},19) and N to ({2},3), D(M,N) has two zero rows and last row

\[
 (0,-4,-4,0,0,0,0,0,0,0).
\]

A word-level lift is the literal product of the retained individual lifts. Its Gamma comparison is strictly commuting. Replacing it by a newly selected lift of the product requires precisely (4.5).

### 4.2 The lift introduces no hidden leading growth

Give the ray-coordinate space the explicitly defined mass norm

\[
 \|z\|_\omega=\sum_j(r_j)_0|z_j|.
\]

This is an auxiliary norm on the cycle-ray coefficients, not a replacement of the original generator-word quotient metric. For any word product \widehat P of nonnegative lifts,

\[
 \|\widehat P\|_\omega
 =\max_j\frac{e_0P r_j}{(r_j)_0}
 =e_0Pv,
\tag{4.7}
\]

where P is the seven-coordinate word product. The upper bound is (3.5); equality is the actual first ray r_1=v. The right side is the original distinct fifth-image count.

For a homogeneous digit set, the original cut has at most four pair representatives. Hence N_(m+n) is between N_m N_n/4 and N_m N_n. Subadditivity and iteration prove rho^m<=N_m<=4rho^m. Equation (4.7) therefore gives

\[
 \rho(\widehat M)=\rho(M)=\rho,\qquad
 \|\widehat M^m\|_\omega\le4\rho^m.
\]

A nontrivial Jordan block on the spectral boundary would contradict this bound. Interior kernel modes and all of (4.4) remain; there is no assertion that the ten-dimensional lift is a linear equivalence.

## 5. An exact all-rank cone dual

Let M_k be the original class of pairs (b,A), with nonempty distinct positive A, b>S(A), and H_2(A) containing no ordered nonzero-step k-AP modulo b. All nonzero steps, including those of small additive order in a composite modulus, are part of the definition. The class is countable; its ranks, integer entries and moduli are not bounded here.

A finite word P=M_1...M_t has reward N(P)=sum_i |A_i|. The empty word has P=I and reward zero. Its actual concatenated generators are distinct because each block sum is below its radix. Its binary image is k-admissible by repeated reduction, lookup of the actual common digit, subtraction, and division. The original fifth-arity count is

\[
 e_0Pv=|H_5(G_P)|.
\]

The inherited cardinality characterization, recalled with its proof in Section 9, gives

\[
 e_0Pv\ge\lambda_k^{N(P)}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

This is the sole arithmetic lower interface used in constructing the dual. It is a proved property of the actual global rate, not a proposed numerical lower estimate.

For 0<u<=lambda_k define on K

\[
\boxed{
 h_u(x)=\inf_{P\text{ finite actual word}}
       \frac{e_0Px}{u^{N(P)}}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

All rows in this infimum are nonnegative. Write one row as ell. By (5.1), ell(v)>=1. Since each v_i<=4, sum_i ell_i>=1/4. The cone inequality x_i>=x_0 therefore proves

\[
 \boxed{x_0/4\le h_u(x)\le x_0.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The upper bound is the actual empty word. A row with sum ell_i>1 is everywhere strictly above that empty-word row at a nonzero x in K. Such a row can be omitted from the infimum, with the explicit comparison ell(x)>=x_0 sum ell_i>x_0 retained. The remaining rows lie in

\[
 \mathcal A=\{\ell\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^7:1/4\le\textstyle\sum\ell_i\le1\}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Consequently h_u is concave, positively homogeneous, coordinatewise monotone on K, and 1-Lipschitz for the specified coordinate sup norm. Indeed every retained row has l1 norm at most one, so the infimum obeys the same Lipschitz estimate. These are auxiliary observation coordinates; no original word metric is changed.

Appending a specified actual matrix M to a word gives

\[
\begin{split}
 h_u(Mx)
 &=\inf_P e_0PMx/u^{N(P)}\\
 &=u^{|A|}\inf_P e_0PMx/u^{N(P)+|A|}
 \ge u^{|A|}h_u(x).
\end{split}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus h_u is an exact all-rank antinorm. The term means a nonnegative concave homogeneous functional on a cone, in the established terminology of Protasov (2021); all the bounds here follow from the displayed original arithmetic rows.

Conversely, suppose a continuous homogeneous function g is strictly positive on K minus zero and satisfies g(Mx)>=u^{|A|}g(x) for every pair in M_k. The compact mass section in Section 3 supplies actual constants 0<c<=C with

\[
 cx_0\le g(x)\le Cx_0.
\]

For a homogeneous block,

\[
 u^{m|A|}g(v)\le g(M^mv)\le C e_0M^mv\le4C\rho(M)^m.
\]

Taking m-th roots yields u<=rho(M)^(1/|A|). The spectral variational formula from Section 9 then gives u<=lambda_k. In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_k=\max\{u>0:\text{a function satisfying (5.3) and (5.5) exists}\}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

The maximum is attained by h_(lambda_k). Equation (5.6) retains every original modular admissibility requirement. A generic matrix preserving K is not automatically an arithmetic member of M_k.

## 6. Quantitative finite rational certificates for every strict threshold

The following result controls the number and height of the *function coefficients*, not the size of the arithmetic block family.

**Theorem C3, quantitative form.** For every integer Q>=137 there is a finite nonempty set

\[
 \mathcal P_Q\subseteq\{p\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^7:\textstyle\sum_i p_i\le Q\}
\]

such that

\[
 g_Q(x)=\min_{p\in\mathcal P_Q}\frac{p\cdot x}{Q}
\]

obeys, with a_Q=1-68/Q,

\[
\boxed{
 a_Q h_{\lambda_k}(x)\le g_Q(x)\le h_{\lambda_k}(x),
\qquad
 g_Q(M(A,b)x)\ge(a_Q\lambda_k)^{|A|}g_Q(x).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

There are at most

\[
 \boxed{\binom{Q+7}{7}}
\tag{6.2}
\]

pieces, and every coefficient has denominator dividing Q and numerator between zero and Q. The constants do not depend on k, |A| or b.

**Proof.** Use the retained rows (5.4) for h_(lambda_k). Round each of their seven coefficients down to its nearest multiple of 1/Q. Let P_Q consist of all resulting integer numerator vectors. It is finite, even though the original rows form an infinite family, and the empty-word row ensures it is nonempty. Counting nonnegative integer vectors of sum at most Q proves (6.2).

For x in K, the actual coordinate budget is sum_i x_i<=17x_0, because sum_i v_i=17. Each rounded row loses at most 17x_0/Q. Therefore

\[
 h_{\lambda_k}(x)-17x_0/Q\le g_Q(x)\le h_{\lambda_k}(x).
\]

Using x_0<=4h_(lambda_k)(x) proves the first inequality in (6.1). In particular g_Q is strictly positive on K minus zero.

For a block of reward n>=1,

\[
 g_Q(Mx)\ge a_Qh_{\lambda_k}(Mx)
 \ge a_Q\lambda_k^n h_{\lambda_k}(x)
 \ge a_Q\lambda_k^n g_Q(x)
 \ge(a_Q\lambda_k)^n g_Q(x).
\]

The last inequality uses 0<a_Q<1 and n>=1. No zero-reward arithmetic block is inserted into this step. This proves (6.1). QED.

It follows that every strict threshold 0<u<lambda_k admits a finite rational certificate. One sufficient order is

\[
 Q\ge137,\qquad Q>\frac{68\lambda_k}{\lambda_k-u}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Since powers of three give lambda_k<=3, the stronger sufficient requirement Q>204/(lambda_k-u) is available. The unknown strict gap is not claimed to have been evaluated. In particular, (6.3) does not supply an effective stopping rule at a conjectured exact endpoint.

The theorem has a precise quantifier order: one finite rational function works simultaneously for *every* actual admissible block at that k. It is stronger than finding a separate finite approximation on each finite dictionary. Conversely, checking that one finite function works still requires the universal arithmetic inequality. Finite dimensionality of the coefficient space does not remove that arithmetic quantifier.

### 6.1 One original block has an exact finite verification problem

Let g(x)=min_i a_i x, with rational nonnegative rows a_i, and let u be rational. For each active row i define the rational cone

\[
 C_i=\{x\in\mathscr K:(a_j-a_i)x\ge0\text{ for all }j\}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
 g(Mx)\ge u^n g(x)\text{ for all }x\in\mathscr K
\iff
 (a_jM-u^n a_i)x\ge0
 \text{ for all }x\in C_i\text{ and all }i,j.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This is obtained by selecting an actual minimizing row at x and then requiring every output row to be at least that minimum times u^n. On the compact section C_i intersect {x_0=1}, it suffices to check its finite rational vertices. A cone containing only zero is vacuous and is retained as such.

Equivalently, each required row belongs to the dual cone generated by the actual inequalities:

\[
 a_jM-u^n a_i
 =\sum_{\nu=1}^{9}\alpha_\nu H_\nu+
   \sum_l\beta_l(a_l-a_i),\qquad\alpha_\nu,\beta_l\ge0.
\tag{6.5}
\]

For rational input the coefficients can be chosen rational. This is the elementary polyhedral dual/Farkas statement: failure of membership separates the row from the finitely generated dual cone, returning an x in the specified primal cone with negative pairing. Conversely, the displayed nonnegative combination immediately proves every pairing nonnegative. Thus both a certificate and a violating source-cone vector have exact finite data.

A failed cone test is a counterexample to the proposed functional. It is not by itself an admissible generator-set counterexample. Section 3 gives approximating finite centrally symmetric numerical sets for cone directions; it does not assert that all such sets are fifth-arity images of admissible generators. An arithmetic improvement requires the original level labels or a separately constructed original block. Section 7 gives such labels in an exact application.

### 9.1 Existence and the finite-image infimum

For k-admissible A and B, put R=2S(A)+1 and C=A union R B. The value map (x,y)->x+Ry is a bijection H_2(A) times H_2(B)->H_2(C). Its inverse is literal Euclidean division. Consecutive second differences in the first coordinate have absolute value at most 2S(A)<R. Divisibility forces them to vanish, so every target progression pulls back to progressions in both factors. A nonconstant target has a nonconstant factor.

The original cost satisfies

\[
 2S(C)+1=(2S(A)+1)(2S(B)+1).
\]

Minimizing that cost at fixed rank is submultiplicative. For a fixed block length l, write n=ql+r with r<l and use repeated composition. Dividing its logarithm by n proves convergence to the infimum of the fixed-length root costs. The comparisons

\[
 2g_k(n)+1\le F_k(n)\le2n g_k(n)+1
\]

give the same limit for g_k. Powers of three are admissible at every rank, so lambda_k exists and lies in [1,3].

For any specified A let H=|H_5(A)|, K=(H-1)/2 and ell=1+floor(log_2(2S(A))). The positive bounded differences sum_a c_a a, c_a in [-2,2], number K and have size at most 2S(A). There is a prime

\[
 p\le256+4K\ell
\]

dividing none of them. One elementary proof uses theta(N)=sum_(p<=N)log p>N/4 for N>=256. The central binomial coefficient divides lcm(1,...,N), giving theta(N)>=(N-1)log2-log(N+1)-sqrt(N)log N>N/4; bounding the decreasing ratios at N=256 verifies the final strict inequality. If all primes through 256+4K ell divided the product of the K differences, its logarithm would both exceed 64+K ell and be less than K ell. This proves the prime assertion.

Reduction modulo p is injective on the actual ternary values H_3(A). A modular binary k-AP therefore has its two adjacent second-difference sides equal as integers. Admissibility forces it constant. Repeated lifting uses the unchanged original weights, with lookup of the unique actual binary digit, subtraction and division; no generator is reduced to its least residue. Every actual generator is nonzero modulo p, which also proves distinctness of all geometric levels.

Now fix A of rank n, put R=4S(A)+1, and form C_t=union_(j<t) R^j A. Its fifth-image count is H^t and its sum is (R^t-1)/4. The same prime construction gives

\[
 p_t\le256+2(H^t-1)t\ell_R,\qquad
 \ell_R=1+\lfloor\log_2 R\rfloor.
\]

Its unchanged-weight lift yields lambda_k<=p_t^(1/(tn)). The factor beyond H^(1/n) is at most

\[

\tag{9.1}
\]
