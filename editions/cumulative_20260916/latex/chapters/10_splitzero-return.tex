\section{1. Two original relation complexes and the actual comparison}\label{n10-two-original-relation-complexes-and-the-actual-comparison}

Fix an oriented finite graph G=(V,E), integer marks t\_v, and the original integral map

\[
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}
$\displaystyle \partial:\mathbb Z^E\to\mathbb Z^V,\quad e_{ij}\mapsto e_j-e_i,
\qquad\ell_t(x)=\sum_vt_vx_v.$
\end{adjustbox}
\]

Let Omega=\{0,1\}\^{}E be the actual binary source. For each admitted subfamily S subset Omega, define

\[
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}
$\displaystyle V_S=\mathbb Z[S],\quad X_S=\partial(S),\quad Y_S=\ell_t(X_S),$
\end{adjustbox}
\]

\[
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}
$\displaystyle q_S:V_S\to\mathbb Z[X_S],\quad e_\epsilon\mapsto e_{\partial\epsilon},
\qquad J_S:\mathbb Z[X_S]\to\mathbb Z[Y_S],\quad e_x\mapsto e_{\ell_tx}.$
\end{adjustbox}
\]

Write B\_S=ker q\_S and K\_S=ker(J\_S q\_S). Their inclusion is literal, and

\[
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}
$\displaystyle \boxed{0\to B_S\to K_S\xrightarrow{q_S}\ker J_S\to0.}$
\end{adjustbox}
\tag{1}
\]

Surjectivity follows by lifting each source basis value x to an actual binary word in its fiber. The kernel is exactly B\_S. Thus the induced map on the degree-zero cohomologies of {[}B\_S -\textgreater{} V\_S{]} and {[}K\_S -\textgreater{} V\_S{]} has the additional kernel K\_S/B\_S=ker J\_S. Source fibers and the extra observation kernel remain separate.

For S subset T the basis inclusions commute with all maps. These are diagrams over the actual join-semilattice of admitted word supports. Applying the original SplitZero reconstruction sends a boundary to its own supported zero. The nonbottom support \{empty binary word\} has a one-dimensional value basis e\_0. The further amplitude map e\_0-\textgreater0 is explicit; it is not external absence.

The free-word relation complex and the graph-chain complex also have an explicit comparison. Let a\_S:Z{[}S{]}-\textgreater Z\^{}E send e\_epsilon to epsilon, and let b\_S:Z{[}X\_S{]}-\textgreater Z\^{}V send e\_x to x. Then

\[
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}
$\displaystyle \partial a_S=b_Sq_S.$
\end{adjustbox}
\tag{2}
\]

For the full source Omega, a\_Omega maps B\_Omega onto ker(partial). To see surjectivity, select a spanning forest. Each nonforest edge gives an integral fundamental cycle with coefficients in \{-1,0,1\}. Its positive and negative binary parts have the same boundary, so their word difference belongs to B\_Omega and augments to that cycle. Subtracting fundamental cycles expresses every integral graph cycle. Consequently

\[
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}
$\displaystyle 0\to B_\Omega\cap\ker a_\Omega\to B_\Omega
\xrightarrow{a_\Omega}\ker\partial\to0.$
\end{adjustbox}
\tag{3}
\]

No equality between these two kernels is asserted. Formula (3) retains the relations among word representatives that the graph amplitude map forgets.

At the graph amplitude level the second observation has its own exact sequence

\[
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}
$\displaystyle 0\to\ker\partial\to\ker(\ell_t\partial)
\xrightarrow{\partial}\ker(\ell_t|_{\operatorname{im}\partial})\to0.$
\end{adjustbox}
\tag{4}
\]

Equations (1)-\/-(4) are the comparison maps between the different source types used in the arithmetic application.

\section{2. A generic disjoint-boundary obstruction}\label{n10-a-generic-disjoint-boundary-obstruction}

If \(c^1,\ldots,c^r\) in \{-1,0,1\}\^{}E have disjoint supports, the same boundary z, and ell\_t(z)!=0, choose the positive part of c\^{}h for h in Q and its negative part otherwise. This defines a map

\[
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}
$\displaystyle \{0,1\}^r\longrightarrow\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb Z$
\end{adjustbox}
\]

with value X+\textbar Q\textbar ell\_t(z). Thus r+1 distinct progression values have original binary witnesses. This applies to any specified free source, boundary map, and scalar observation, not only graphs. The full proof and exact graph specialization are in Section 3 of \texttt{graphical-proof-provider.md}.

For graphs, edge-disjoint paths are disjoint lifts of e\_v-e\_u. Their original scalar increment is t\_v-t\_u. For a complete graph on m marked vertices there are m-1 such paths. They generate an m-term progression, explaining the critical (m+1)-term threshold in the complete-graph construction.

\section{3. Root-budget prolongation retains the whole original defect}\label{n10-root-budget-prolongation-retains-the-whole-original-defect}

For the complete graph K\_m, the actual tournament-score image is

\[
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}
$\displaystyle \Sigma_m=\{\rho-\partial\epsilon:\epsilon\in\Omega\},
\qquad\rho=(0,1,\ldots,m-1).$
\end{adjustbox}
\]

If r is a nonzero integral vector of coordinate sum zero with

\[
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}
$\displaystyle r(U)\le|U|(m-|U|)\quad(U\subseteq V),$
\end{adjustbox}
\tag{5}
\]

the new theorem constructs actual binary words whose m+1 score rows x\_0,...,x\_m satisfy

\[
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}
$\displaystyle \boxed{x_1-x_0=e_j-e_i,\qquad
\Delta(x_0,\ldots,x_m)=(0,\ldots,0,r),}$
\end{adjustbox}
\tag{6}
\]

where i maximizes r and j minimizes r. The construction retains r itself and does not divide it by its gcd or replace it by a support label.

The proof constructs an integral endpoint allocation on K\_m plus an additional K\_(m-2). Its cut inequalities are deduced from (5) by four exact membership cases. The resulting correction is t-s=r+(e\_j-e\_i), with s\_i=0 and s\_j=m-1. Reversing the m-1 disjoint j-i paths gives the original root string through s, and appending t gives (6). Section 5 of the proof provider contains all cut bounds, the integral flow construction, and the binary path maps.

Every difference of two actual score points satisfies (5). Therefore a scalar collision ell\_t(r)=0 between distinct score classes produces an (m+1)-term progression. The original source defect r is nonzero, while its receiving scalar defect is zero; the nonzero scalar increment t\_j-t\_i makes the resulting progression nonconstant.

This is a constructive obstruction map, not a declaration that the original cohomology class is absent. It uses the exact additional kernel in (1) and (4) to produce the forbidden arithmetic witness.

\section{4. A supportwise cohomology equivalence forced by admissibility}\label{n10-a-supportwise-cohomology-equivalence-forced-by-admissibility}

Assume the complete-graph marks are distinct, its positive edge weights are distinct, and its binary numerical image has no nonconstant (m+1)-AP. The previous theorem proves that ell\_t is injective on the full boundary image X\_Omega. It follows on every admitted word support S that J\_S is a bijection on its actual value bases.

Consequently

\[
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}
$\displaystyle \boxed{B_S=K_S\quad\text{for every }S\subseteq\Omega,}$
\end{adjustbox}
\tag{7}
\]

and the maps induced by J\_S give a natural isomorphism of the two quotient diagrams in (1). The inverse sends e\_y to the unique original e\_x with ell\_t(x)=y. On reconstructed totals the map preserves the original support, its coefficient zero, and global absence; the scalar semiring is the inherited G(Z). This is a genuine diagram/cohomology statement for the specified finite binary source, proved uniformly for every graph order m.

At the integral edge-coordinate level the corresponding bounded statement is

\[
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}
$\displaystyle \ker(\ell_t\partial)\cap\{-1,0,1\}^E
=\ker\partial\cap\{-1,0,1\}^E.$
\end{adjustbox}
\tag{8}
\]

The full linear kernel in (4) retains its additional m-2 rational dimensions. At the doubled value source, further collisions can also occur. The new ten-generator example has 3081 doubled score classes and 2103 numerical ternary classes; the additional free-value kernel has dimension 978. Its addition square is retained in the proof provider. Thus (7) is not extended without proof to a higher coefficient box, tensor observation, or the original infinite theta source.

\section{5. An all-rank obstruction with its quantitative return}\label{n10-an-all-rank-obstruction-with-its-quantitative-return}

At the critical progression length m+1, every admissible complete-graph projection is score-injective by (6). There are at least m! transitive score classes. Therefore every canonical radix for such a block is at least m!, even when its ruler changes at every level. With n\_m=binom(m,2), this gives the exact lower rate (m!)\^{}(1/n\_m).

A prime p\textgreater m and marks (0,1,p,...,p\^{}(m-2)) give a valid modular construction in base p\^{}(m-1), hence upper rate p\^{}(2/m). Bertrand\textquotesingle s bound p\textless=2m and the elementary lower estimate log(m!)\textgreater=m log m-m+1 show that the optimum Xi\_m throughout this complete critical source family satisfies

\[
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}
$\displaystyle \boxed{\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{m\log\Xi_m}{\log m}=2.}$
\end{adjustbox}
\tag{9}
\]

This is a matching leading asymptotic for a specified unbounded-rank family. It does not bound the unrestricted Erdős 817 rate from below. Its implication is that further large-rank improvement must alter the critical source geometry, rather than attempt to collapse these particular score classes: their collapse has the explicit forbidden packet (6).

For the metric calculation extend the displayed free integral presentations to real coefficients; their free basis quotients and specified maps are retained. The source metric stays equally explicit. For the orthonormal binary-word basis, if eta(x) is the number of words with boundary x, the first quotient metric is diag(1/eta(x)). If mu(y)=sum\_(ell\_t x=y)eta(x), the original minimum-norm quotient metric at the numerical image is diag(1/mu(y)). The sections are the actual averages in each word fiber. These formulas follow by completing the square in each finite fiber and commute with the original quotient maps.

For the new base-1651 block the largest multiplicity is 24, so its t-level tensor return to unit value coordinates has norm 24\^{}(t/2). No uniform arithmetic gain is inferred after omitting this factor. On smaller supports the same formulas use their own retained fiber counts.

\section{7. Canonical monotone dual and finite obstruction compiler}\label{n10-canonical-monotone-dual-and-finite-obstruction-compiler}

The second proof provider, \texttt{monotone-obstruction-dual.md}, constructs the exact future value

\[
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=.92\linewidth}
$\displaystyle F_u(R)=\inf_{p\text{ safe from }R}P(p)/u^{N(p)}.$
\end{adjustbox}
\]

For u\textless=lambda\_k it lies in {[}1/2,1{]}, is inclusion-monotone, and H\_u=2F\_u is the greatest bounded Bellman subsolution. For u\textgreater lambda\_k it vanishes at every support, through explicitly represented positive-cost paths. A finite path with 2P\textless u\^{}N, followed by the retained zero reset, produces an actual modular-safe block of rate below u. The general pattern-matrix version retains reset cost R\_L and coefficient interval {[}1,R\_L{]}.

The false state remains active throughout this limiting calculation. A zero coefficient in the future-value limit has its supported-zero meaning, not a missing source label. Finite graph closure and finite source packets are tools to control that problem, not claims that the arithmetic inequalities over all blocks have already been evaluated.
